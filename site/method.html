What it is

Robot Security Radar is a daily-updated public radar of security and safety incidents across embodied robots—humanoids, quadrupeds, and consumer/service robots. It is built for buyers: prospective buyers, procurement teams, operators, and anyone evaluating these products. Security researchers and journalists are secondary audiences.

AI-generated summaries are clearly labeled on each incident card and should always be verified against the linked primary source. This site is not security or legal advice. All claims are sourced from publicly available information and should be verified with the linked sources.

How the Radar works

Each day, a pipeline pulls articles from publicly available sources: news outlets, security press, robotics press, communities, and vulnerability databases. A deterministic filter screens out anything that is not about a specific robot or robot platform combined with a security or safety event. Surviving articles are reviewed by an AI screening step that discards everything that is not a discrete, verifiable incident—podcasts, opinion pieces, marketing hype, and speculation never pass. The AI clusters related articles into single Incidents, assigns a status and date, and writes a short buyer-facing summary. Results are published as a static site with an RSS feed, updated daily.

Limitations

  • Automated filtering can miss relevant coverage if articles avoid the expected keyword combinations.
  • AI summaries can be wrong. Treat them as a convenience and verify at the linked source.
  • Incident clustering can mis-group articles or create duplicates.
  • Absence of evidence is not proof a vendor has not fixed an issue—a vendor may release a fix that the radar has not yet detected.
  • Source feed outages or changes surface as failed pipeline runs visible in the GitHub Actions log.
